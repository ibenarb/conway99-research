Independent results
The lattice step without packing bounds
The lattices L₀ of §4.3 (rank 15, determinant 2⁸, elementary discriminant group, integral norms on the dual) fall into exactly 31 isometry classes, and each class has seven pairwise orthogonal dual units. This proves (11) without the packing step and without Lemma 4.2.
Every such lattice is {v ∈ V : (v, c) even for all c ∈ C} for a unimodular V of rank 15 and a 4-space C of V/2V on which the norm vanishes modulo 4, and conversely. Counting the pairs (V, C) in two ways gives the mass.
\sum_{[L_0]} \frac{1}{|\mathrm{Aut}(L_0)|} \;=\; \frac{N\, m_{15}}{2295} \;=\; \frac{116980081}{898948399104000}, \qquad N = 3876193035,\quad m_{15} = \frac{29713}{385648863215616000}
Here N counts the totally singular 4-spaces, 2295 the Lagrangians of L₀*/L₀, and m₁₅ is the mass of the five unimodular lattices. I found 20 classes from the 20 doubly-even codes in ℤ¹⁵ and 11 more from ℤ⁷ ⟂ E₈. Their masses add up to the target exactly. Sampling the other three unimodular lattices gave nothing new, as the mass requires.
The atlas of §5.1 then becomes an intrinsic test on X = L₀/2L₀*. For each class and each of the eight vertices of K₈ I asked for two norm-5 vectors in each of the 21 required classes, pairing at most 1 with every dual unit, such that every dual unit meets exactly 14 of the 42.

Two remarks on the first link.
• The exponent bound b ≤ 1 and the exclusion of groups of order six in Theorem 7.3 are not needed. Only the restriction of the prime divisors is used.
• Without the computer search the chain does not close. Makhnev–Minakova give a group order dividing 2·3³·7·11, and Cesarz–Woldar reduce the case of 7 to a group of order 7 but do not exclude it. The published exclusion of orders 7 and 11 is therefore load-bearing.
Earlier results taken over
On 9 and 10 November I reviewed Djematene's manuscript in the version dated 23 September 2026 (file preuve_c3.tex, unchanged in the repository since 11 October; SHA-256 4db92733…). I checked the fixed-point-free case line by line, condensed it to seven steps, and tested its mechanism on 24 real graph actions. I had followed the counting lemma for at most three fixed points. I had checked the fixed-triangle case (his Section 4) only in part and had not looked at the Lean sources, which were not public then.
So my earlier verdict covered the fixed-point-free case in full. For the remaining case it relied on Behbahani and Lam.
Checked now
• Section 4, the fixed triangle. All four points verified by hand. The fixed coordinates of every invariant integral 3-eigenvector are divisible by 3, because the last three rows of 11(Ã + 4I) − 2·jwᵀ vanish modulo 3. The weighted Gram matrix is integral with determinant 7ᵃ·11ᵇ. Both lattices reduce to bases of the same kernel over 𝔽₃, so the determinant is 1 modulo 3. The norm 99 of the all-ones vector forces b odd, so the determinant is 2 modulo 3. The step r = r′, which the text passes over with "word for word", holds: r ≤ r′ because the reduced basis is independent inside the kernel, and r′ ≤ r because 2(Q̄ + I) has rank r′ and factors through an r × r matrix.
• Lemma 7, at most three fixed points. Rechecked in full. My own count agrees: a fixed vertex has 2a fixed neighbours with a in {1, 4, 7}, the fixed set has 1 + 2a² vertices, and the case of 33 is impossible.
• Scope of the formal statement. In the pinned Lean revision 9678dec the file Challenge.lean states, for Mathlib's IsSRGWith 99 14 1 2, that every automorphism σ with σ³ = 1 is the identity. That is the full statement, with or without fixed points. The Lean sources (3,871 lines in all) contain no sorry outside that file, where the statements are deliberately left unproved, and no added axiom or native_decide. The archived logs record acceptance by the Lean kernel and five further checkers, locally and in GitHub Actions.
• The link to the group order. Statement and proof structure of Crnković–Maksimović, Section 7, as in the table.
Not done: a Lean rebuild, a replay of the Behbahani–Lam search, a review of Zhang's order-three route. His route treats the fixed-point-free case and takes the absence of fixed points from Behbahani's thesis, so it is supporting evidence for one case only.
Verdict and remaining conditions
The paper proof of the order-three exclusion is complete and correct as far as I can check it by hand. Together with the archived formal verification this suffices to use it as a cited result, provided its status is stated: an unpublished manuscript that its own title page marks as not yet read by a third party.
The asymmetry conclusion then holds under three conditions.
1.  Theorem 1.1 of the manuscript, with the qualifications of this report (three searches in one implementation, the singleton-root search the least supported).
2.  Djematene's exclusion of order 3.
3.  The published computer search excluding automorphisms of orders 7 and 11.
Two concrete residual items, neither a precondition for citing:
• One Lean rebuild at the pinned revision (lake exe cache get, lake build, lake comparator --paranoid). It would turn archived logs into a reproduced formal check.
• Five log files at the pinned revision do not match SHA256SUMS (the two local comparator logs, the local con-leche log, lean_challenge_solution and lean_install). All Lean sources and the GitHub Actions log match. The list should be regenerated or the difference explained.
Proposed wording
The sentence under review is correct. I suggest naming the inputs in the same breath:
Using Theorem 1.1, Djematene's exclusion of automorphisms of order three, and the published computer search of Behbahani and Lam that leaves 2 and 3 as the only possible prime divisors of the group order, every strongly regular graph with parameters (99,14,1,2) is asymmetric. Whether such a graph exists remains open.
Replacement for the paragraph after Corollary 1.2:
Corollary 1.3 (conditional on the cited results). A strongly regular graph Γ with parameters (99,14,1,2), if one exists, has trivial automorphism group.
Derivation. By the computer search of Behbahani and Lam [B; BL], the order of Aut(Γ) has no prime divisor other than 2 and 3; Crnković and Maksimović [CM, Theorem 7.3] record this as |Aut(Γ)| = 2ᵃ3ᵇ. Corollary 1.2 gives a = 0. Djematene [D] proves that Γ has no automorphism of order three, with or without fixed points, so b = 0 by Cauchy's theorem.
The three inputs differ in standing. The exclusion of the primes 7 and 11 is a published computer search that we have not replayed. Djematene's result is a hand proof dated 23 September 2026 with a public Lean 4 formalization of the full statement; it has not appeared in a refereed publication. In this project its paper proof was checked in full and the formalization was not rebuilt. Theorem 1.1 is the computer-assisted result of the present paper. Zhang's separate computer-assisted exclusion of fixed-point-free automorphisms of order three (18 November 2026) supports one case of [D] and is not used here.
For the abstract, in place of the sentence that the result is not a nonexistence proof:
Combined with Djematene's exclusion of automorphisms of order three and the published restriction of the prime divisors of the group order, a graph with these parameters would be asymmetric. Its existence remains open.
For the attribution paragraph:
The exclusion of order two is due to Zhang and is the subject of this paper. The exclusion of order three is due to Djematene and predates it. Zhang's later route to the fixed-point-free order-three case is a separate, supplementary result. None of the three has completed external peer review.
